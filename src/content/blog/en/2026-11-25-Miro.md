# Your Own Pokémon on a Miro Board
As part of the 2026 summer semester, we jointly designed and ran our own workshop at CoderDojo Schöneweide.
At the beginning of the semester, we were welcomed into CoderDojo and received plenty of ideas, information, and support. This quickly enabled us to plan and implement our own workshop independently. Even without much prior experience, we were able to actively participate in CoderDojo.
For our workshop, we decided to introduce the children to collaborative work using a digital whiteboard. To do this, we used Miro, a tool that allows multiple people to work simultaneously on a shared digital canvas.
The children were tasked with developing their own creative ideas in groups and bringing them to life together. The theme of Pokémon and fantasy creatures served as inspiration. The children were completely free to decide how they wanted to design their ideas. This resulted in custom Pokémon cards, drawings of personal interests, fantasy creatures, and many other creative outcomes.

## Preparation
Before the workshop could begin, we had to prepare a few things first. We clarified the technical requirements so that all the children could participate without any issues.
To ensure a quick start, we created various templates and a cheat sheet on the Miro board and familiarized ourselves thoroughly with the digital whiteboard tool. This allowed us to better support the children during the workshop and show them the most important features.

## The Workshop
Right on cue for Berlin's new heat record of 37.4 degrees Celsius, our preparations were complete and the workshop was ready to start.
Despite the high temperatures, we were delighted to welcome a total of ten children and teenagers who listened attentively and worked enthusiastically on their ideas that day. Light snacks like fruit and vegetables, along with plenty of fans, also helped make the summer heat more bearable.
After just a short time, the children were able to use Miro independently. It was especially wonderful to see how they discussed things with one another, exchanged ideas, and supported each other. The children helped each other out, made suggestions, and developed their ideas further together.
At the end, the groups presented their results and told the other children what they had created. It became clear just how diverse and creative the outcomes were. Each group approached the task in its own unique way and showcased its individual ideas.

## Our Experiences
During the workshop, we noticed that the children got the hang of Miro very quickly. After a brief introduction, they were able to work independently and use the tool for their own ideas.
At the same time, we realized that the introduction to a workshop plays a crucial role. It wasn't always easy to motivate all the children right away and create a smooth transition into the actual task.
For future workshops, we would therefore focus the beginning more on movement and getting to know each other. A game like "Fruit Salad" could help quickly create a relaxed atmosphere and make it easier for the children to get started.

## Conclusion
In closing, the three of us are very happy to have gotten to know CoderDojo Schöneweide and to have run our own workshop.
The experience showed us that digital tools like Miro offer a great way to foster creativity and collaboration. The focus isn't on the tool itself, but rather on developing ideas together and having the ability to make them visible.
We can recommend to anyone interested—whether parents, children, or future mentors—to simply stop by CoderDojo Schöneweide and get in touch with the great team.
